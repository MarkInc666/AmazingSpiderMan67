ASM67 — REMAINING 17 MODES
RESEARCH AND STARTING-POINT CONCEPTS

Research draft: August 12, 2026
Project baseline: August 11 full codebase plus the completed Doctor Atlantean patch


HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

This is a source-backed idea book, not a final rules document. Episode facts are
separated from design proposals. Each recommendation is intended to begin a
mode-by-mode discussion. Nothing here should be counted as an approved design or
coded until it has been reviewed.

The core test for every concept is whether a player can quickly understand what
to shoot, what changed, and why the rule belongs to that particular character.

1. DEVARGAS
Chapter 9 — Lost Worlds
Episode: “Cloud City of Gold” (November 30, 1968)
Current status: Implemented / needs machine playtest

CONFIRMED EPISODE MATERIAL

Peter’s plane crashes in the Andes. Spider-Man leads the survivors through a
jungle, a whirlpool and underground river, and a cave filled with vampire bats.
They reach the hidden Cloud City of Gold, ruled by DeVargas, a descendant of
15th-century conquistadors. DeVargas uses a giant golden eagle to attack the
group and captures Spider-Man’s companions. Spider-Man also encounters a giant
spider near the volcano. DeVargas fires a cannon into the volcano while trying
to kill Spider-Man, triggers an eruption, and destroys his own city. Spider-Man
rescues the captives and escapes before the city is consumed.

STRONG THEMATIC HOOKS

- A dangerous expedition with distinct locations
- The hidden golden city as a destination rather than a starting point
- Three memorable defenses: golden eagle, giant spider, and DeVargas’s cannon
- Captured companions who must be rescued
- The villain’s own attack starting a volcanic catastrophe
- An escape sequence instead of a conventional final knockout

APPROVED DESIGN — CITY OF GOLD SCORE ATTACK

DeVargas is a single-ball pure score attack. The player receives 20 timed gold
opportunities, or 25 with More Jackpots. Opportunities are randomly selected
from 13 repeatable shot groups. A group may repeat later but cannot be selected
as the immediately following opportunity, and a currently lit group cannot be
selected again.

Shot groups: left web, center web, left pop, right pop, left 3-bank, right
5-bank, A rollover, B rollover, middle A, middle B, all three upper targets as
one group, main spinner, and upper spinner. Any target or the rear rubber scores
a lit bank. Banks are not reset when selected but reset normally when completed.

Each opportunity pulses gold at three speeds. Base timing is 4 seconds slow,
3 seconds medium, and 3 seconds fast. More Time changes the phases to 5, 4, and
4 seconds. A new opportunity appears every 8 seconds, so opportunities overlap
naturally. Collecting a lit shot immediately replaces it and restarts the
8-second selection interval.

Base values are 150K slow, 125K medium, and 100K fast. Bigger Jackpots changes
them to 200K, 175K, and 150K. Every award scores immediately and adds its full
value to the persistent devargas_bonus bank shown as DEVARGAS GOLD.

Safety Net starts a 10-second ball save. Shot Assist automatically collects the
first opportunity that expires and awards the fast-tier value. The background
GI is light blue 8080D0. Lit groups pulse between gold F0F060 and dim gold
303000 at one, two, or four pulses per second. The rooftop gate opens only while
the upper-target group or upper spinner is lit and closes when neither remains.

The mode ends successfully after all opportunities have resolved or on drain.
Previously earned gold remains banked. The summary shows Mode Points, DeVargas
Gold, and Gold Shots collected.

2. CHARLES CAMEO
Chapter 10 — Savage Oddities
Episodes: “The Sinister Prime Minister” and “Double Identity”
Current status: Placeholder

CONFIRMED EPISODE MATERIAL

Charles Cameo is an actor and master impersonator based loosely on the
Chameleon. In “The Sinister Prime Minister,” he kidnaps and replaces a visiting
prime minister, uses a gas-firing cane, steals a large gold payment, and attempts
to escape by airplane before Spider-Man exposes him. In “Double Identity,” he
impersonates Peter Parker and later Spider-Man while stealing art treasures.
Brutus helps with the robberies. The confrontation between the real and false
Spider-Man produced the familiar pointing image.

STRONG THEMATIC HOOKS

- One identity copying another
- Mirrored or paired shots
- A real action immediately followed by its imitation
- Stolen art and evidence
- Success through exposing the fake, not simply hitting a strong villain
- A final false-Spider-Man confrontation

PLAYFIELD TRANSLATION

- The playfield has natural left/right pairs: webs, pops, banks, and upper exits.
- A successful shot can cause Cameo to “copy” it on the opposite side.
- Art treasures can be represented by saucers after an impersonation is exposed.
- The upper playfield or VUK can be reserved for the final getaway rather than
  used throughout the mode.

CONCEPT A — DOUBLE IDENTITY / MIRRORED SHOTS (RECOMMENDED)

One source shot is lit. Hitting it causes Cameo to copy that identity: the
corresponding shot on the opposite side lights for a short exposure window.
Hitting the copy exposes Cameo and completes the round. Examples could include
left web to right web, left pop to right pop, left bank to right bank, or left
exit to right exit.

Complete several impersonations. The final round represents Cameo disguised as
Spider-Man: two equivalent Spider-Man shots appear, and the player must make a
two-shot confrontation ending at the VUK or a designated web target.

This is not a hidden-shot guessing mode. The player creates the copy by making
the first shot, then deliberately shoots the mirror. That keeps it distinct from
Mysterio’s clues, Pardo’s spinner reveal, and Fiddler’s memorized note sequence.

CONCEPT B — COLLECT THE EVIDENCE

Three suspects are lit. Ordinary shots gather evidence and progressively remove
innocent identities until only Cameo remains. Shoot the exposed suspect to
arrest him. This is thematically sound but too close to an investigation/clue
mode and risks repeating Mysterio.

CONCEPT C — STOLEN FACES

Cameo steals the identity of the last major shot the player made, temporarily
turning that shot into a trap. The player must shoot a different category to
break the disguise. This is mechanically novel but could be difficult to read
and explain during fast play.

CASE FILE STARTING POINTS

- More Jackpots: add an additional impersonation before the final confrontation.
- Bigger Jackpots: increase each exposure and final award.
- More Time: extend the mirrored-shot exposure window.
- Safety Net: 10-second ball save.
- Shot Assist: the first expired mirror window counts as completed.

OPEN QUESTIONS

- Which left/right pairs are reliable enough to use?
- Does an expired copy window reset only the round or reduce prior progress?
- Should the final false Spider-Man confrontation use the two web targets?
- Should “The Sinister Prime Minister” supply a separate airplane escape phase?

3. BRUTUS
Chapter 10 — Savage Oddities
Episode: “Double Identity”
Current status: Placeholder

IDENTITY CLARIFICATION

This roster slot is Charles Cameo’s human bodyguard from “Double Identity,” not
Doctor Vespasian’s invisible dog, also named Brutus. Cameo’s Brutus knocks down
Spider-Man, assaults the officer transporting the valuable tapestry, takes the
officer’s place, and protects Cameo during the art theft.

STRONG THEMATIC HOOKS

- A bodyguard who blocks access rather than running the scheme
- Physical interference and short openings
- Luring the guard away from something valuable
- Art treasures and the disguised tapestry delivery
- Escalating difficulty as Brutus recovers faster

PLAYFIELD TRANSLATION

- A lit saucer can be the current art treasure.
- A bank on the opposite side can lure Brutus away.
- The player then has a short window to reach the unguarded treasure.
- Pops and rubbers can score physical “bashing” points without becoming the
  entire objective.

CONCEPT A — GET PAST THE BODYGUARD (RECOMMENDED)

One of the three saucers holds a valuable art treasure, but Brutus is guarding
it. A clearly lit opposite-side bank or web target lures him away. Once that shot
is made, the treasure saucer lights for a brief steal/recovery window. Collect
the treasure before Brutus returns.

Repeat for three treasures. Later rounds require either more lure hits or shorter
windows. A shot into a guarded saucer scores a small blocked award and ejects
quickly without collecting the treasure. Completing all treasures defeats
Brutus by making his guarding irrelevant.

This gives a minor physical character a clear routing mode instead of another
Rhino-style health bar.

CONCEPT B — BRUTUS BASH

Pops, slings, rubbers, and drops reduce Brutus’s strength until a knockout shot
lights. This is easy to understand but overlaps Rhino and other bash modes.

CONCEPT C — TAPESTRY DELIVERY

The player must move a tapestry through three delivery checkpoints while Brutus
randomly intercepts one. Spinner activity reveals the safe route. This uses the
episode well but risks becoming another reveal mode.

CASE FILE STARTING POINTS

- More Jackpots: add a fourth high-value artwork.
- Bigger Jackpots: increase treasure values.
- More Time: lengthen each unguarded window.
- Safety Net: 10-second ball save.
- Shot Assist: automatically lures Brutus away on the first guarded round.

OPEN QUESTIONS

- Should treasures always progress Saucer 1 to 3, or change position?
- Should a guarded saucer hit cost time, points, or only opportunity?
- How many lure hits should later rounds require?

4. SKYMASTER
Chapter 10 — Savage Oddities
Episode: “Criminals in the Clouds” (October 5, 1968)
Current status: Temporary placeholder requiring a distinct non-rooftop design

CONFIRMED EPISODE MATERIAL

Skymaster kidnaps football star Roy Robinson to ransom him to his scientist
father, inventor of an invisibility serum. Skymaster hides a militaristic
airship inside a cloud that does not move with the others. Spider-Man notices
the stationary cloud, boards the airship, rescues Roy, defeats the crew, and
causes the flying base to crash into the harbor.

DESIGN CONSTRAINT

Baron Von Rantenraven already owns the rooftop limited-flips Sky Harbor assault.
Skymaster should therefore emphasize camouflage, tracking, kidnapping, and the
airship’s gas cells on the lower playfield rather than becoming another rooftop
air-fortress mode.

STRONG THEMATIC HOOKS

- One stationary cloud hidden among moving clouds
- Tracking and revealing a camouflaged airship
- Damaging balloon or gas cells
- Rescuing a hostage
- A final crash into the harbor

CONCEPT A — FIND THE STATIONARY CLOUD (RECOMMENDED)

Several lower-playfield shot groups represent clouds and move or pulse in a
simple repeating pattern. One cloud remains stationary for the current cycle.
Hitting the stationary shot finds Skymaster’s airship and lights a timed damage
shot elsewhere. Damage three gas cells, with the stationary cloud changing
after each success.

After the airship is crippled, a saucer lights to rescue Roy and the VUK or a
web shot lights to crash the airship safely into the harbor.

The player is watching movement rather than guessing a hidden answer. This uses
the episode’s best clue without copying Pardo’s spinner reveal or Electro’s
random traveling spark.

CONCEPT B — CLEAR THE CLOUD COVER

Pop and sling hits reduce a cloud-cover meter. When the meter reaches zero, the
airship appears at one of three shots for a short attack window. Cloud cover
then rebuilds. This is readable but adds another fill-and-drain meter mode.

CONCEPT C — RANSOM RESCUE

Collect three pieces of tracking information, lock onto the airship at a saucer,
then complete a timed hostage rescue. This is straightforward but underuses the
stationary-cloud imagery.

CASE FILE STARTING POINTS

- More Jackpots: add a bonus gas cell or a post-rescue airship jackpot.
- Bigger Jackpots: increase gas-cell and rescue awards.
- More Time: extend the damage and rescue windows.
- Safety Net: 10-second ball save.
- Shot Assist: the first cloud cycle clearly holds or automatically identifies
  the stationary cloud.

OPEN QUESTIONS

- Which three or four shot groups can create a readable moving-cloud pattern?
- Should a wrong cloud hit merely score small points or accelerate the pattern?
- Is Roy rescued at a saucer or the VUK?

5. SIR GALAHAD
Chapter 11 — Dimensional Finale
Episode: “Knight Must Fall” (April 26, 1970)
Current status: Placeholder

CONFIRMED EPISODE MATERIAL

Sir Galahad is a modern criminal knight who behaves as if he is the genuine
Arthurian figure. He rides a motorcycle as his “trusty steed” and uses an
electrified lance. He initially stops a box-office robbery, then takes the money
himself. He later robs an armored car because he claims its money was stolen.
Finally, he attacks a shipment carrying a sword believed to be Excalibur.
Spider-Man defeats him in a waterfront joust and knocks him into the water.

STRONG THEMATIC HOOKS

- Repeated lance charges
- Left/right jousting exchanges
- Electricity as a warning state
- A motorcycle charge rather than a medieval horse
- Three escalating encounters ending with Excalibur
- Galahad’s warped belief that his thefts are acts of justice

CONCEPT A — THE WATERFRONT JOUST (RECOMMENDED)

Play three short jousting rounds: box office, armored car, and Excalibur. Each
round begins with Galahad charging from a clearly indicated side. The player has
a few seconds to make the opposite-side defense shot, then a center counter-shot.
Success wins the exchange; failure lets Galahad score a hit but restarts the
same round rather than erasing the entire mode.

The third round requires several alternating left/right exchanges before the
final web or VUK shot knocks Galahad into the waterfront. The electrified-lance
warning can be shown with a brief GI or insert pulse confined to physically
compatible lamps.

This creates a directional reaction mode distinct from Baron’s limited flips,
Cyclops’s flip economy, and Diana’s staged drop hunts.

CONCEPT B — STEAL EXCALIBUR

Galahad moves among several robbery shots. The player intercepts him three times
and then protects Excalibur at the upper playfield. This is simpler but resembles
a generic traveling-shot chase.

CONCEPT C — LANCE CHARGE METER

Galahad’s lance charges continuously. Spinner and target hits drain it while
drops build Spider-Man’s counterattack. This underuses the joust and overlaps
other meter-management modes.

CASE FILE STARTING POINTS

- More Jackpots: add a fourth bonus joust after Excalibur.
- Bigger Jackpots: increase won-exchange and final awards.
- More Time: extend each reaction window.
- Safety Net: 10-second ball save.
- Shot Assist: automatically blocks the first missed charge.

OPEN QUESTIONS

- Which physical shots should represent left defense, right defense, and the
  center counterattack?
- How many exchanges should the Excalibur joust require?
- Does a failed defense reduce accumulated round progress?

6. MASTER VINE
Chapter 11 — Dimensional Finale
Episode: “Vine” (November 16, 1968)
Current status: Placeholder

CONFIRMED EPISODE MATERIAL

Peter inadvertently releases a giant destructive vine from a seed found in a
missing scientist’s home. He uses the scientist’s time machine to travel to
3,000,000 B.C. The only way to stop the present-day vine is to obtain radium
gems from the idol in a Forbidden City occupied by intelligent plant creatures.
Master Vine captures Spider-Man and forces him to fight the giant caterpillar-
like Goliath in an arena. Spider-Man defeats Goliath, takes the two radium eyes,
and returns to the present. Feeding the vine too much radium overloads and kills
it.

STRONG THEMATIC HOOKS

- A present-day threat solved by traveling into the distant past
- Vine growth spreading across the playfield
- A time machine as the transition between two rule states
- Arena combat with Goliath
- Two radium-gem eyes
- Winning by overfeeding the menace rather than starving it

CONCEPT A — TWO ERAS, ONE VINE (RECOMMENDED)

Present phase: a vine-growth timer begins. The vine spreads into playfield areas
one at a time. Spinner progress powers the time machine before too many areas are
overgrown.

Prehistoric phase: the rules and lighting change to the Forbidden City. Defeat
Goliath with a compact arena objective, then collect the two radium gems from
two clearly indicated shots. Completing the pair returns the player to the
present.

Final phase: feed the two gems to the giant vine through a designated center or
web shot. One gem weakens it; the second overloads it and wins the mode. Growth
continues during the present phases, so the player is racing an understandable
threat without juggling independent zone timers.

This is a genuine time-travel mode with two contrasting states, rather than an
area-control copy of Metal-Eating Monster or Clive and Blotto.

CONCEPT B — RADIAL VINE GROWTH

The vine begins at the center and grows along several branches. Hit the tip of a
branch to prune it while gathering radium. Prune every branch, then overfeed the
root. Visually strong, but it may require a more complicated light map.

CONCEPT C — FORBIDDEN CITY ARENA

Skip the present-day growth and make the whole mode a limited-flips arena battle
against Goliath, Master Vine, and the radium idol. This is compact but overlaps
the existing limited-flip modes and discards the episode’s best structure.

CASE FILE STARTING POINTS

- More Jackpots: each radium gem qualifies a separate gem jackpot before the
  final overload.
- Bigger Jackpots: increase arena, gem, and overload awards.
- More Time: slow the present-day vine-growth timer.
- Safety Net: 10-second ball save.
- Shot Assist: prune the first growth step or supply part of the Goliath goal.

OPEN QUESTIONS

- What event should represent entering and leaving the time machine?
- Should overgrown areas merely show danger, suppress scoring, or cause failure
  when a fixed number is reached?
- Which two shots best read as the idol’s radium eyes?
- Is the rooftop useful as the Forbidden City, or can the two-era effect remain
  entirely on the lower playfield?

7. THE SPIDER-MEN
Chapter 11 — Dimensional Finale
Episode: “Home” (December 14, 1968)
Current status: Placeholder

IMPORTANT STORY INTERPRETATION

The alien Spider-Men, including Carol and her father, are not conventional evil
villains. Their civilization crash-landed on Earth and has lived underground
while waiting for a ray from its home planet. Carol steals equipment because a
proton-device test will destroy her people, who are highly vulnerable to proton
rays. Spider-Man initially believes the theft is criminal, then learns the truth,
stops the proton test, and helps the civilization return home.

The mode should therefore be a rescue/cooperation story rather than “defeat the
alien Spider-Men.”

STRONG THEMATIC HOOKS

- A false accusation that becomes an alliance
- Alien Spider-Men with powers resembling Spider-Man’s
- Stolen equipment needed to prevent a catastrophe
- An underground spacecraft and hidden civilization
- A proton countdown
- A homeworld ray arriving at the climax

CONCEPT A — STOP THE PROTON TEST (RECOMMENDED)

Begin with a visible proton countdown. Collect three equipment components from
three different shot families. Deliver each component to the underground base,
represented by a saucer or the VUK. The countdown resets or gains time with each
successful delivery but never disappears completely.

After all components are installed, open the route to the upper playfield and
align the homeworld ray with the three upper targets. Completing the alignment
before the proton timer expires sends the Spider-Men home and wins the mode.

This produces a clear story arc: apparent theft, equipment collection, delivery,
and dimensional departure. It is not a multiball fight and it does not copy the
Fly Twins simply because multiple spider-like characters appear.

CONCEPT B — SPIDER-POWER COOPERATION

Start two-ball multiball with one ball representing Spider-Man and one Carol.
Paired shots must be completed close together to stop the device. This is a
strong metaphor but ordinary pinballs are visually indistinguishable, making the
rule harder to communicate reliably.

CONCEPT C — FIND THE HIDDEN CIVILIZATION

Use a search-and-reveal first act, then a short proton-device shutdown. This is
faithful but risks repeating clue and hidden-shot modes.

CASE FILE STARTING POINTS

- More Jackpots: add a homeworld-ray jackpot after each aligned upper target.
- Bigger Jackpots: increase component, delivery, and home awards.
- More Time: extend the proton countdown.
- Safety Net: 10-second ball save.
- Shot Assist: collect or deliver the first missing component automatically.

OPEN QUESTIONS

- Which shot receives each component, and where are components delivered?
- Does the countdown start immediately or after the first component?
- Should upper access remain open only for the final alignment?
- What happens if the proton timer expires: immediate mode failure or loss of
  one component and a shortened recovery chance?

8. MASTERMIND TRAP
Chapter 2 — Masked Masterminds
Villains: Doctor Octopus, Mysterio, Scorpion, Lizard, Parafino
Current status: Functional framework exists, but the design is not approved

CHAPTER IDENTITY

These villains control, deceive, poison, transform, or physically trap their
victims. The wizard should feel like Spider-Man has been caught inside a layered
plan, not like five unrelated villains have returned for five small rematches.

CONCEPT A — ESCAPE FIVE LAYERS (RECOMMENDED)

Start as a protected single-ball escape mode. A capture meter advances at a
steady pace. The player dismantles five trap layers, each expressed as a short,
very readable rule:

- Mechanical restraint: complete rollover or bank control points.
- Illusion chamber: make the one visibly stable shot while the others move.
- Venom lock: build and discharge venom through the spinner and a target.
- Transformation serum: collect and deliver an antidote.
- Wax prison: heat and break the final shell.

Every cleared layer stops the capture meter briefly and permanently opens more
of the playfield. Escaping the fifth layer begins a short two- or three-ball
revenge multiball in which the five former trap shots become Jackpots. The mode
is won by escaping; multiball is the celebration and scoring finish.

Why it works: the chapter becomes one escalating imprisonment story. Individual
villain themes are visible, but their full regular-mode rules are not replayed.

CONCEPT B — FIVE TRAP NODES

Start multiball with five trap areas active simultaneously. Complete each before
its independent timer expires. This is energetic, but it resembles the current
Crime Wave framework and several area-defense modes.

CONCEPT C — MASTERMIND SEQUENCE

The game displays a five-villain order and the player repeats it through mapped
shots. This is easy to explain but too close to Fiddler’s Simon Says structure.

KEY REVIEW QUESTIONS

- Should clearing all five layers be required, or should survival through a
  fixed time count as completion?
- Does the revenge multiball affect chapter progression or only scoring?
- How punitive should the capture meter be after a missed objective?


9. CRIME WAVE
Chapter 4 — Crime Wave
Villains: The Plotter, Fly Twins, Fifth Avenue Phantom, Enforcers, Doctor Cool
Current status: Substantial three-ball framework exists, but it is not yet an
approved finished wizard

CHAPTER IDENTITY

This chapter is a coordinated citywide series of thefts: plans and blueprints,
jewelry robberies, shrunken valuables, gang activity, and frozen diamonds. A
crime-wave wizard should be a pursuit across several active crimes, with the
player deciding what can be stopped before the getaway.

CONCEPT A — FIVE ESCALATING HEISTS (RECOMMENDED)

Start three-ball multiball. Five heists occur one at a time in a randomized but
non-repeating order. Each heist has its own playfield area and a short getaway
timer. Completing the area captures that villain and scores a Jackpot. If the
timer expires, that villain escapes with the loot; the next heist still begins.

After all five heists have been attempted, the VUK lights for the Crime Wave
Jackpot. Its value is based on captured villains, recovered loot, balls still in
play, and the chapter Case File bonus. Capturing all five lights an additional
Super or add-a-ball opportunity.

Why it works: it is a citywide chase with meaningful successes and failures,
rather than five anonymous areas being maintained simultaneously. Every run can
finish, but a perfect run remains special.

CONCEPT B — FIVE TIMED AREAS

Retain the current framework: all five villain areas run independently, with
upper exits collecting Jackpots. This may prove good on the machine, but it must
be tested for readability before being accepted as the final design.

CONCEPT C — RECOVER THE MASTER PLAN

Every villain holds one piece of the Plotter’s master blueprint. Complete all
five shot groups, then assemble the plan through the spinner and collect at the
VUK. Strong narrative, but mechanically close to a standard five-area checklist.

KEY REVIEW QUESTIONS

- Should escaped villains remain escaped or return for another attempt?
- Does multiball end the wizard immediately at one ball, or is there a short
  single-ball getaway grace period?
- Can the best parts of the current implementation be retained inside the
  sequential-heist structure?


10. THE WEB TIGHTENS
Chapter 5 — Danger Lurks
Villains: Harley Clivendon, Conquistador, Spider-Slayer, Metal-Eating Monster,
Fiddler
Current status: Placeholder

CHAPTER IDENTITY

The five threats have little literal connection, but each forces Spider-Man to
track a danger that is initially difficult to contain: hypnosis, a jungle hunt,
an expanding robotic search, destruction spreading through the city, and a
memorized musical pattern. The wizard name suggests Spider-Man should build a
containment network that tightens around all five.

CONCEPT A — BUILD THE WEB NETWORK (RECOMMENDED)

Start two-ball multiball. Five web links connect opposite or separated parts of
the playfield. Each link requires a first shot to anchor the web and a second
shot within a short window to complete it. Completed links remain locked in and
visibly form a five-part containment web.

Every incomplete link represents one chapter villain and has a small escalating
penalty or deadline. The player can choose which link to finish next. Complete
all five links to trap the threats, light the VUK, and collect the Tightened Web
Super Jackpot. If multiball falls to one ball, allow a brief chance to complete
the current link before the wizard ends.

Why it works: the mode uses Spider-Man’s own power as the chapter culmination.
It unifies unrelated villains without replaying their rules and gives the title
a literal, visible meaning.

CONCEPT B — DANGER CASCADE

One new threat activates every several seconds. Hit its area before the next
one appears. This is intuitive but too similar to the Metal-Eating Monster’s
existing overlapping attack timers.

CONCEPT C — HUNT THE FIVE SIGNALS

The spinner traces five villain signals and the player shoots each revealed
location. This is clear but overuses reveal mechanics already found elsewhere.

KEY REVIEW QUESTIONS

- Which five shot pairs make strong, achievable web links?
- Do completed links award Jackpots immediately or only build the Super?
- Can a missed link window be retried without losing completed links?


11. FIFTH DIMENSION CURSE
Chapter 6 — Mystic Menace
Villains: Pardo, Fantastic Fakir, Kotep, Super Swami, Infinata
Current status: Functional three-ball framework exists, but the design is not
approved

CHAPTER IDENTITY

This chapter combines hypnosis, illusion, sorcery, demons, false realities, and
the literal Fifth Dimension. The wizard should make the playfield feel unstable
while keeping the player’s immediate objective readable.

CONCEPT A — FIVE FALSE REALITIES (RECOMMENDED)

Start three-ball multiball. Reality shifts among five villain states on a fixed
timer. Each state changes the GI presentation and lights a small, unique group
of shots. One “true” objective is always visibly stable while illusion shots
pulse or move. Hitting the true objective breaks that reality permanently and
scores a Jackpot. A false hit scores little and speeds the next shift but does
not erase completed realities.

After all five realities are broken, the VUK opens as the portal to Infinata.
Collect the Fifth Dimension Super to win. The chapter Case File bonus is added
only to Jackpots and the Super.

Why it works: the entire playfield feels cursed and unstable, but each moment
still gives the player one clear truth. It is more cohesive than six independent
GI timers and broader than any one villain’s illusion rule.

CONCEPT B — CURSED SEALS

Five seals require ordered shot pairs. Breaking a seal causes the next one to
appear with a different color and rule. This is clear but may feel like five
ordinary stages rather than a Fifth Dimension crisis.

CONCEPT C — DIMENSIONAL COLLAPSE

GI regions disappear one by one while the player relights them. This makes a
strong show but overlaps Super Swami and the current wizard framework.

KEY REVIEW QUESTIONS

- How many seconds should each reality remain active?
- Should realities cycle automatically or only after a meaningful shot?
- What visual convention clearly distinguishes the true objective from an
  illusion without relying on colors that physical lenses cannot show?


12. MAD SCIENCE MELTDOWN
Chapter 7 — Mad Science
Villains: Dr. Noah Boddy, Dr. Magneto, Professor Pretorius, Doctor Dumpty,
Dr. Von Schlick
Current status: Placeholder

CHAPTER IDENTITY

The chapter contains invisibility, magnetism, shrinking technology, balloons and
gas, and an oil-stealing weapon. A generic laboratory explosion would waste that
variety. The strongest shared story is five unstable experiments drawing power
from one overloaded reactor.

CONCEPT A — CONTROLLED MELTDOWN (RECOMMENDED)

Start three-ball multiball with a reactor heat meter and five experiment
stations. Hitting an experiment advances its unique objective and also increases
reactor heat. Spinner activity vents heat. If the reactor reaches maximum before
all five experiments are disabled, the current experiment resets or one ball is
removed from play; the exact penalty requires testing.

Once all five experiments are disabled, the goal reverses: the player must now
intentionally push the reactor to maximum and shoot the VUK during a short red
meltdown window. That controlled final overload destroys the lab and scores the
Super Jackpot.

Why it works: the player first prevents a meltdown and then deliberately causes
one at the safe moment. That reversal gives the wizard a memorable identity and
makes the spinner a strategic control rather than just a value builder.

CONCEPT B — FIVE FAILED EXPERIMENTS

Complete five ordinary areas, each with a visual mutation. This is easy to code
but risks becoming another chapter checklist.

CONCEPT C — SHRINKING LAB

The playable scoring area contracts as the mode proceeds, leaving fewer valid
shots until the player restores full size. Highly thematic for Pretorius, but it
does not represent the other four scientists evenly.

KEY REVIEW QUESTIONS

- What is the fair consequence of an early heat maximum?
- Which shots vent heat, and can over-venting have a cost?
- How should the five experiments differ without recreating five full modes?


13. NATURE STRIKES BACK
Chapter 8 — Elemental Chaos
Villains: Clive and Blotto, Doctor Zapp, Bolton and Boomer, Snowman, Plutonians
Current status: Placeholder

CHAPTER IDENTITY

The threats combine darkness/erasure, camera flashes, lightning, an electrically
charged snow creature, and widespread freezing. Several elements interact in
the episodes: electricity animates or harms creatures, flashes expose threats,
and conductive webbing drains power. A good wizard can make those elements
affect one another instead of presenting five separate jackpots.

CONCEPT A — BALANCE THE ELEMENTS (RECOMMENDED)

Start three-ball multiball with four linked meters: darkness, electrical charge,
snow, and deep freeze. Each major shot reduces one meter but raises or activates
another. For example, an electrical attack can expose Blotto but energize the
Snowman; draining the Snowman can feed the camera-flash supply; heat from a flash
can weaken deep freeze.

The objective is to bring every meter into its safe zone at the same time. Doing
so stabilizes nature and lights a VUK Jackpot. Repeat for a small number of
increasingly difficult cycles, then collect a final Super.

The status display must remain extremely simple—four short bars or named danger
states—because the underlying interaction is intentionally richer than a normal
mode.

CONCEPT B — ELEMENTAL CHAIN REACTION

Complete a fixed sequence in which one villain’s element defeats the next. This
is easier to teach but could become a rigid linear checklist.

CONCEPT C — FIVE WEATHER FRONTS

Five areas deteriorate on timers and must be restored. This overlaps Metal-
Eating Monster, Plutonians, and other zone-defense rules.

KEY REVIEW QUESTIONS

- Are four linked meters readable on the existing display during multiball?
- Which interactions are intuitive enough to teach with callouts?
- Should one successful balance win, or should there be three stabilization
  cycles with increasing speed?


14. INVASION FROM EVERYWHERE
Chapter 9 — Lost Worlds
Villains: Dr. Manta, Igor, Doctor Atlantean, DeVargas, Molemen
Current status: Placeholder

CHAPTER IDENTITY

The threats reach Manhattan from an enslaved island, a fortified lost world,
Atlantis, the Cloud City of Gold, and the underground Molemen civilization. The
chapter title promises simultaneous pressure from several directions. A simple
five-area multiball would duplicate the Molemen and Crime Wave concepts.

CONCEPT A — FIVE-FRONT TUG OF WAR (RECOMMENDED)

Start three-ball multiball. Five invasion fronts begin outside the city and each
has a short three- or four-step track toward the center. Their paired shots push
them one step back; periodic timers move one or more fronts forward. Repeatedly
attacking the same front can drive it completely off the map and lock it out as
defeated.

If a front reaches the city center, it captures a ball in one of the saucers or
disables a major scoring area until the player makes a rescue shot. The mode is
won by pushing all five fronts back to their origins, then shooting the VUK for
the Invasion Super.

Why it works: progress can move in both directions. The player is not merely
checking five boxes or waiting for five independent timers. The rule expresses
an actual battle line and supports the “from everywhere” title.

CONCEPT B — VERTICAL INVASION

Use the eight horizontal GI bands as a shared city map, with underground and
undersea forces rising while aerial forces descend. Visually strong, but it
would reuse Doctor Atlantean’s defining water-band presentation too directly.

CONCEPT C — LOST-WORLD PORTALS

Five portals open in sequence and release a short villain objective. Close all
five and then destroy the master portal. Clear, but close to replaying miniature
versions of the regular modes.

KEY REVIEW QUESTIONS

- Which front maps to which shot without confusing the five simultaneous states?
- Does a captured ball remain held, or is it quickly ejected after a rescue
  requirement is established?
- Can the mode remain fair if two fronts advance on the same timer tick?


15. WHO IS THE REAL VILLAIN?
Chapter 10 — Savage Oddities
Villains: Charles Cameo, Brutus, Desperado, Skymaster, Conner’s Reptiles
Current status: Placeholder

CHAPTER IDENTITY

This deliberately strange roster includes disguises, a bodyguard, a futuristic
cowboy, a camouflaged airship, and intelligent reptiles. Charles Cameo provides
the narrative glue: one apparent villain is Cameo in disguise, and Spider-Man
must eliminate the genuine suspects before confronting the impostor.

CONCEPT A — THE IMPOSTOR HUNT (RECOMMENDED)

At mode start, one of the other four villains is secretly selected as Cameo’s
current disguise. Four suspect areas are available. Completing a suspect’s short
test produces a definitive result: genuine villain or Cameo. Genuine villains
are captured and removed. Finding Cameo starts a final chase in which he changes
through the remaining identities on successive shots.

The game should not require blind guessing. Every completed suspect test always
advances the investigation. Finding Cameo early increases the final value; if he
is the fourth suspect, the player still reaches the confrontation through steady
progress.

This can run as two-ball multiball to make the chapter wizard feel larger, but
the investigation should remain readable with one ball after a short grace
period.

CONCEPT B — TWO SPIDER-MEN

Use two-ball multiball as a literal version of the Cameo pointing confrontation.
Paired shots reveal which ball represents the real Spider-Man. Because physical
balls are indistinguishable, this is visually funny but mechanically unreliable.

CONCEPT C — SAVAGE GAUNTLET

Fight all five villains in a rapid random order. Straightforward, but it does
not justify the wizard’s excellent title.

KEY REVIEW QUESTIONS

- Should the hidden disguise be truly random or rotate evenly between games?
- What compact test best represents each of Brutus, Desperado, Skymaster, and
  the reptiles?
- Does finding Cameo immediately end the investigation or begin a required
  multi-identity final chase?


16. TIME-TOSSED SHOWDOWN
Chapter 11 — Dimensional Finale
Villains: Sir Galahad, Master Vine, Master Technician, The Spider-Men,
Baron Von Rantenraven
Current status: Placeholder

CHAPTER IDENTITY

This chapter ranges from Arthurian delusion and prehistoric plant civilization
to anti-gravity Manhattan, alien Spider-Men, and a World War I-style sky
fortress. The wizard should feel like eras and worlds are colliding, not simply
like another all-shots multiball.

CONCEPT A — STABILIZE FIVE TIME RIFTS (RECOMMENDED)

Start three-ball multiball. One time rift opens at a time for a fixed interval.
Each rift has a compact two- or three-shot pattern based on its world: joust,
prehistoric growth, anti-gravity machinery, alien home ray, or sky attack.

Completing a rift stabilizes it permanently and scores a Jackpot. An expired
rift becomes fractured and returns later with a shorter window or one additional
shot. The player eventually sees all five; success does not depend on a lucky
order. Stabilize all five, then shoot the VUK to return Manhattan to the correct
time and score the Super Jackpot.

Why it works: the time shifts are the main rule, and the five villains supply
brief visual identities rather than full replayed modes. Failed rifts create
escalation without deleting completed progress.

CONCEPT B — CHOOSE THE ERA

Spinner hits rotate among five eras, and the player shoots the corresponding
objective to lock it. This offers agency but could be difficult to control in
multiball and may overemphasize the spinner.

CONCEPT C — TIME COLLAPSE COUNTDOWN

All five eras attack simultaneously while a master countdown runs. This is
dramatic but likely too dense and too similar to multi-area defense modes.

KEY REVIEW QUESTIONS

- Should fractured rifts become harder, less valuable, or both?
- Does the wizard require all five, or can four stabilized rifts qualify a lower
  final award?
- How long should each rift remain active during three-ball play?

17. KINGPIN — FINAL SHOWDOWN
Episodes: “King Pinned” and “The Big Brainwasher”
Current status: Existing city-area framework; dedicated final design/retheme
remains

CONFIRMED EPISODE MATERIAL

In “King Pinned,” Kingpin runs a counterfeit medicine operation. Frederick
Foswell is his informer inside the Daily Bugle. Kingpin kidnaps J. Jonah Jameson,
nearly unmasks Spider-Man with gas from his cane, and plants a time bomb in the
Bugle’s printing presses. Spider-Man rescues Jameson and races to save the Bugle,
but Kingpin escapes.

In “The Big Brainwasher,” Kingpin operates a nightclub where Mary Jane takes
photographs of city officials. The camera draws them into a back room containing
a powerful brainwashing machine. Kingpin traps Spider-Man in a chained room that
fills with water. Spider-Man uses a web-air bubble, escapes, rescues Mary Jane and
the officials, and defeats Kingpin.

The two episodes naturally form a final-wizard escalation: expose the criminal
empire, survive the trap, save the Bugle, free the city, and finally capture the
Kingpin who escaped the first confrontation.

CONCEPT A — KINGPIN’S EMPIRE FALLS (RECOMMENDED)

Use a cinematic four-act finale rather than one uninterrupted generic multiball.

Act 1 — Expose the Empire. Major shots represent Kingpin’s network. Chapter
completion and Case Files determine values or advantages, but the player does
not replay all eleven chapter wizards. Break several criminal-network nodes and
collect the evidence at the Daily Bugle VUK.

Act 2 — Rescue Jameson / Escape the Water Trap. Kingpin captures Jameson and
traps Spider-Man. Begin a tense protected single-ball sequence: break restraints,
create the web-air bubble, and make the escape shot before the water timer ends.
The exact shot pattern should be short and unmistakable.

Act 3 — Save the Bugle. A visible printing-press bomb countdown begins. Spinner
activity runs the presses or exposes bomb wiring; the three upper targets or
three saucers can represent the circuits that must be disabled. A final VUK shot
defuses the bomb and launches the climactic multiball.

Act 4 — The Big Brainwasher. Start a large multiball. Five city-official areas
are under Kingpin’s control. Completing an area frees its officials and scores a
Jackpot. Freeing all five lights a Kingpin Super at the VUK or a deliberately
chosen final shot. Collect it to defeat Kingpin and complete the game. Remaining
balls can continue into victory laps after the story victory.

WHY THIS SHOULD REPLACE THE CURRENT CLEAR-THE-CITY STRUCTURE

The existing framework already supplies useful multiball, area, saucer, gate,
and VUK infrastructure. Its weakness is thematic: it is a generic city-clearing
mode. The recommended structure preserves reusable technical pieces for Act 4
while giving Kingpin an episode-specific narrative and a true final-act climax.

CONCEPT B — ELEVEN-CHAPTER GAUNTLET

Replay one shot for every completed chapter, then fight Kingpin. This recognizes
the entire game but risks becoming long, repetitive, and difficult to read.

CONCEPT C — KINGPIN TAKEOVER MULTIBALL

Begin immediately with five controlled city areas and clear them all. This is
closest to the existing code and simplest to implement, but it does not feel
like the reward for completing the entire game.

PROGRESSION AND REWARD STARTING POINTS

- Every completed chapter wizard should provide a visible advantage, value, or
  safety resource, not another qualification requirement.
- Total Case Files can increase Jackpots or grant one limited recovery feature.
- The final wizard should have checkpoints between major acts so an early drain
  does not force an excessively long replay from the beginning; the exact
  continuation policy requires approval.
- Story victory should occur on the final Kingpin Super. Victory laps are a
  scoring reward and should not delay recognition of completion.

OPEN QUESTIONS

- How many acts should survive a drain or continue to the next ball?
- Which act should receive the largest multiball and longest ball save?
- Should failure in the water trap or bomb sequence end the wizard, reset that
  act, or consume a previously earned chapter resource?
- How should the eleven chapter completions affect the finale without producing
  eleven separate rule exceptions?
- What final physical shot best represents actually capturing Kingpin?

FINAL RESEARCH CONCLUSION

The strongest starting concepts are the ones built around a single episode-
specific action that no other ASM67 mode owns:

- DeVargas turns Cloud City into a rapid gold-collection score attack.
- Cameo copies the player’s shot on the opposite side.
- Brutus must be lured away from guarded artwork.
- Skymaster hides in the one cloud that does not move.
- Galahad turns left/right shot reactions into a joust.
- Master Vine forces play between the present and 3,000,000 B.C.
- The Spider-Men must be rescued from the proton test and sent home.
- Each wizard uses one chapter-level system rather than replaying five villains.
- Kingpin receives an episode-based, multi-act finale with the existing area
  multiball infrastructure reserved for the climactic Brainwasher act.

DeVargas and The Spider-Men are approved and coded. The remaining entries are
research starting points until each receives a separate design review. The next
recommended discussion is Invasion from Everywhere, followed by Charles Cameo.
