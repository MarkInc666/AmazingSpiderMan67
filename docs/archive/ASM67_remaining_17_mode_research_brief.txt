ASM67 — REMAINING 13 MODES
RESEARCH AND STARTING-CONCEPT BRIEF

Prepared: August 12, 2026


PURPOSE

Research the remaining 13 incomplete or unapproved ASM67 modes and develop a strong,
episode-based starting concept for each one. These concepts are intended for
later discussion and refinement. They are not approved final designs and should
not be coded until reviewed with the user.

Chapter mini-wizards: 11 total; 2 approved/coded; 3 functional but provisional;

Remaining research/design workload: 13 modes.

RESEARCH REQUIRED FOR EACH REGULAR VILLAIN

For each of the three remaining regular villains:

1. Identify the character and every relevant 1967 Spider-Man episode.
2. Summarize what the villain does in the episode.
3. Separate confirmed episode facts from interpretation or design invention.
4. Identify the villain's strongest visual, story, and action themes.
5. Explain how the villain fits the assigned chapter.
6. Translate the episode actions into physical pinball possibilities using the
   real ASM67 playfield.
7. Propose two or three substantially different gameplay directions.
8. Recommend the strongest direction and explain why it is distinct.
9. Identify possible overlap with existing villain modes and how to avoid it.
10. List the important design questions that still require user decisions.


RESEARCH REQUIRED FOR EACH CHAPTER WIZARD

For each of the nine unfinished chapter mini-wizards:

1. Review the five villains and the chapter's shared story identity.
2. Identify episode events, threats, locations, or imagery that can unify them.
3. Decide whether the current wizard name still fits the best concept.
4. Propose two or three possible wizard structures.
5. Recommend one structure that feels like a culmination of that chapter.
6. Explain how each of the chapter's five villains is represented.
7. Avoid simply replaying five individual villain modes in miniature.
8. Consider whether the wizard should use multiball, staged single-ball play,
   limited flips, escalating timers, area control, or a hybrid structure.
9. Identify how prior chapter accomplishments could affect the wizard without
   making it impossible for weaker players.
10. List the decisions that must be reviewed before a final design is approved.

Case File rule for every chapter mini-wizard: do not apply the five individual
regular-villain powers. Add 20K per collected chapter Case File only to each
Jackpot and Super Jackpot (0–25 files; maximum +500K). Do not add it to ordinary
switch, spinner, target, progress, or other non-jackpot scoring.


RESEARCH REQUIRED FOR KINGPIN — FINAL SHOWDOWN

1. Research Kingpin's role and actions in the 1967 animated series.
2. Identify the strongest episode-specific finale material.
3. Explain how Kingpin can credibly serve as the game's ultimate adversary.
4. Develop a finale that reflects progress through the full game rather than a
   generic city-clearing framework.
5. Propose multiple structures for the final wizard.
6. Recommend a structure with a clear opening, escalation, climax, and victory.
7. Consider how completed chapters, defeated villains, Case Files, rooftop play,
   saucers, drops, webs, spinner, VUK, and multiball can contribute.
8. Keep the rules understandable during play despite the mode's large scope.
9. Identify any qualification, continuation, or failure rules that need user
   approval.


EXPECTED ENTRY FOR EACH MODE

Each mode's research entry should contain:

- Mode name and chapter
- Current status
- Character or chapter background
- Relevant episode title or titles
- Concise episode plot and confirmed actions
- Strong thematic hooks
- Available playfield translations
- Concept A
- Concept B
- Optional Concept C when genuinely distinct
- Recommended starting concept
- Why the recommendation fits the source material
- Why it is distinct from existing ASM67 modes
- Possible scoring/progression framework at a high level
- Lighting, display, sound, and presentation opportunities
- Likely Case File interactions for regular villains
- Risks, conflicts, and implementation concerns
- Questions requiring user review
- Sources used


ASM67 DESIGN CONSTRAINTS

- Use the 1967 animated series as the primary thematic source.
- Preserve the approved physical playfield layout and device behavior.
- Do not redesign already approved modes merely to make a new concept fit.
- Avoid duplicating an existing mode's main gameplay identity.
- Consider the balance of timed modes, limited-flip modes, multiballs, linear
  objectives, accuracy modes, survival modes, and area-control modes.
- Use rooftop access and the upper playfield only when the episode concept
  supports it; not every remaining villain should become another rooftop mode.
- Respect the physical light and lens limitations defined in config/lights.yaml.
- Use rooftop_diverter_open and rooftop_diverter_close for gate control.
- Keep Case File behavior meaningful and specific to each regular villain.
- Do not treat existing functional wizard shells as approved finished designs.
- Do not write code during this research pass.


RESEARCH QUALITY RULES

- Verify obscure characters and episode events with sources rather than memory.
- Prefer original episode information and reliable episode references.
- Clearly label uncertain or conflicting facts.
- Do not invent episode events and present them as factual.
- Use episode events as inspiration without forcing every plot beat into rules.
- Concepts should be concrete enough to begin a design conversation, but should
  leave scoring values and detailed rules open unless a value is essential to
  explaining the idea.


DELIVERABLE

Produce one master research document covering all 13 modes. Organize it first by
chapter and then by mode. Include a short comparison table at the end showing:

- Recommended gameplay identity
- Ball count
- Primary shots or devices
- Timer or flip limit
- Main risk or failure condition
- Closest existing mode
- How duplication is avoided

After the master research document is reviewed, finalize the modes one at a
time through discussion. Only then should approved designs be coded and added to
the project documentation and planning workbook.
